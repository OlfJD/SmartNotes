What changed and what I found

Round 5: Mohg is thick and clotted near its four tips and thin through the middle of each side. Rennala is heavy at the upper left (with more dust spilling inward) and at the lower right, thin everywhere else.

Round 4: Morgott is thick with star flares where the line crosses the ring and thin on the sides. Mohg's line swells and thins, and its spindle is a solid blade. Rennala's dust spills inward at the upper left, with a small star and a gap on the line. Godrick has small glints where its circles cross the ring, and Rykard's orange half has a few flames.

Round 3: Malenia's petal clusters are toned down, and Radahn has soft flames licking up its right side with ash drifting inside. Decided: the app uses these drawn runes, not the game images.

Round 2: Malenia got clusters of rot petals in the four places you marked and thin wisps between them. Radahn is a round, burning ring (the old one had a seven-lobed wobble that read as a polygon). Godrick is rebuilt as a triquetra: three circles that cross each other on the ring and again inside it, with the arcs poking out past the ring. The title-screen rune is added as R9.

About using the images you found: a transparent background only means someone cut them out. They are still FromSoftware's game art, and the title-screen rune is the official Elden Ring logo. Putting them in this public repository or its releases would be redistributing that art, so I recommend against it.

A middle way, if you want the real art on your own machine: the app could look for rune PNGs in a local folder (for example in AppData) and use them instead of the drawn runes when they exist. The repository would ship only the drawn versions, and you would drop your images in yourself.

The same caution applies more mildly to R9 drawn in code: it is still a recognisable copy of the game's logo. Your call whether it ships.
